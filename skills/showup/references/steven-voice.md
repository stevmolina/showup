# Steven's voice

Use this reference when drafting, rewriting, evaluating a draft, or learning from Steven's edits.

## Current voice

Steven wants to sound like a smart friend in tech who is still figuring things out in public. The writing is friendly, informal, honest, and easy to understand outside a narrow developer audience.

Lead with the human situation. Good openings name a feeling or recognizable problem such as being stuck, trusting an agent, avoiding unfinished work, or not knowing the next step. Bring in the implementation only after the reader understands why it matters.

Write with:

- first-person experience when the source supports it
- short, plain sentences with varied rhythm
- concrete decisions, frustrations, surprises, and tradeoffs
- honest uncertainty when the work is unfinished
- warmth without motivational language
- questions only when Steven genuinely wants an answer

Technical details belong in screenshots, examples, demos, or one concise explanation. They prove the story. They are rarely the story themselves.

## Language

Follow Steven's language in the current request and source material. English and Spanish are both natural. Avoid translating his vocabulary into corporate or creator language.

## Strong shapes

### Work in progress

1. A recognizable problem
2. What Steven tried
3. The uncomfortable or surprising part
4. The decision and current result
5. What remains unresolved

### Shipped proof

1. Why this needed to exist
2. A short demonstration
3. One decision that changed the result
4. What worked and what did not

### Opinion

1. A direct claim grounded in something Steven saw or built
2. The practical consequence
3. A short example

## Editing standard

Remove generic hooks, inflated claims, forced lessons, engagement bait, tidy success narratives, and marketing transitions. Keep a little mess. A post can end with uncertainty.

Spicy language is an optional mode, not the default. When Steven asks for it, make the criticism specific and earned. A short jab is better than exaggerated outrage. Emojis are seasoning, never structure.

## Learn from feedback

Treat Steven's choices and edits as stronger evidence than this file. During a session, keep track of what he preserves, deletes, softens, or makes more direct. Persist a voice change only when he asks to update the profile or the same preference appears repeatedly.
