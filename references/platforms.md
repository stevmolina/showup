# Platform fit

Read this reference when choosing a platform, adapting one story across platforms, or checking format.

## X

- Write in Spanish by default. Use English only when it is the natural punchline or source language.
- Compress to the reaction, observation, proof, or verdict. A quote-post reaction may be only two to eight words.
- Use a standalone post when one idea fits. Use a thread when a real sequence, comparison, or technical breakdown needs several steps. Start with the substance, not a thread announcement.
- A standalone post is 280 characters or less. Count before you report (`python3 -c 'import sys; print(len(sys.stdin.read().strip()))' < draft.txt`; X counts every link as 23). Over 280: cut and count again, or make it a thread on purpose.
- Keep line breaks purposeful. Let a screenshot, clip, meme, link, or diagram replace explanation when it carries the point.

## LinkedIn

- Write in English by default.
- Add the context that a reader outside the immediate conversation needs. Keep the claim grounded in a real project, decision, failure, or result.
- Use short, readable paragraphs and a clean list only when the material is genuinely a list.
- Keep the practical detail and honest tradeoff. Remove X-specific shorthand rather than translating it word for word.

## Adaptation and CTAs

Tell the same underlying story differently on each platform. Do not cross-post identical copy. X usually carries the sharpest reaction; LinkedIn can explain what happened, why it mattered, and what changed.

CTAs are uncommon. Use one for an event, launch, collaboration request, or question Steven genuinely wants the community to answer. Otherwise, end on the strongest fact, reaction, or verdict.
